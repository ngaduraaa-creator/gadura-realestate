Hicksville Property Taxes Explained (Nassau County)

How Nassau assessments and school taxes work for Hicksville owners

Property taxes are the single biggest carrying cost for Hicksville homeowners, and they are higher on Long Island than in most of the country. Understanding them matters whether you're buying, or pricing a home to sell.

How Nassau taxes are built

Your bill combines Nassau County, Town of Oyster Bay, and — the largest piece — the Hicksville Union Free School District. Nassau assesses homes at a fraction of market value and applies a tax rate to that assessed value, so two similar homes can carry very different bills depending on assessment history.

Assessment vs. market value

The county's assessed "full market value" often runs well below actual sale prices — Hicksville homes recently sold around $779,533 (Redfin, May 2026). That gap is normal in Nassau; don't rely on the assessment to price a sale.

Grieving your assessment

If your assessment looks high relative to comparable Hicksville sales, you can file a tax grievance with Nassau County to seek a reduction. There is a filing deadline each year. See our Long Island property-tax grievance guide.

STAR and exemptions

Owner-occupants should confirm they have the STAR exemption (and Enhanced STAR if 65+). Veterans and other exemptions may also apply. These reduce the school-tax portion of your bill.

On Long Island the largest part of the bill is the local school district — for Hicksville, the Hicksville Union Free School District — on top of Nassau County and Town of Oyster Bay taxes.

Can I lower my Hicksville property taxes?

If your Nassau assessment is high relative to comparable Hicksville sales, you can file a tax grievance each year to seek a reduction. There is an annual filing deadline.
