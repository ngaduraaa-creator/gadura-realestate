Section 8 Landlord Guide — Queens, NY

What Queens owners need to know about renting to a household that uses Section 8 (Housing Choice Voucher): how the program pays, the steps, and the law. Sources are linked below.

Can I refuse a renter who has a Section 8 voucher? No. New York State and New York City law bar a landlord from refusing a renter because of a lawful source of income, which includes Section 8 vouchers, CityFHEPS and other public assistance (Executive Law §296(5); NYC Admin. Code §8-107; New York State Attorney General, Residential Tenants' Rights Guide, checked November 18, 2026). Apply the same written criteria to every applicant.

How the program pays

The Housing Choice Voucher program, also called Section 8, helps eligible households rent in the private market. The household typically pays 30 percent of its adjusted monthly income toward the rent, and the housing agency pays the rest to the owner; that payment is called the Housing Assistance Payment (NYCHA, About Section 8, checked November 18, 2026).

In New York City, vouchers are administered by the New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA) and by the Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD). Ask the renter which agency issued the voucher; the paperwork, the unit inspection and the payments go through that agency.

Steps for an owner, in general

  1. Screen the applicant with the same written criteria you use for everyone.
  2. Complete the agency's request-for-tenancy paperwork with the renter, including the proposed rent.
  3. The agency inspects the unit and decides whether the proposed rent can be approved.
  4. Sign the lease with the renter and the payment contract with the agency.

Owners of HPD-subsidised tenancies can register for HPD's owner portal to see inspection schedules, results and payment histories, and can list available apartments for HPD voucher holders through the listing service HPD names on its Section 8 owners page (checked November 18, 2026). Each agency publishes its current payment standards, the most it will approve for each unit size; check them with the agency before you set the rent, because they change.

Screening fairly

  • Write your criteria down and apply them the same way to every applicant.
  • Do not advertise “no vouchers”, “no programs” or anything similar, and do not ask voucher holders for more than other applicants.
  • Before a lease is signed, the most you can charge an applicant for a credit and background check is $20.
  • You cannot turn an applicant down because of a past legal case with a landlord.

Source for the last two points: New York State Attorney General, Residential Tenants' Rights Guide, checked November 18, 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I refuse a Section 8 voucher holder?

No. New York State and New York City law bar landlords from refusing a renter because of a lawful source of income, including Section 8 vouchers (Executive Law §296(5); NYC Admin. Code §8-107).

Who pays what under Section 8?

The household typically pays 30 percent of its adjusted monthly income toward the rent, and the housing agency pays the rest to the owner (NYCHA, About Section 8, checked November 18, 2026).

How much rent will the program approve?

Each agency publishes payment standards and reviews the proposed rent for the specific unit. Check the current figures with NYCHA or HPD; this page does not publish them because they change.

Sources, all checked November 18, 2026: NYCHA, About Section 8; HPD, Section 8 owners; NY Attorney General, Residential Tenants' Rights Guide.
