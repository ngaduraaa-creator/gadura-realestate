Changing brokerages in New York: how it actually works

The single most useful thing to understand before you give notice: in New York you cannot move your own licence. Your current broker has to release you first, and only then can your next broker pick you up.

Everything below is from the Department of State, the OneKey® MLS rulebook, or REBNY’s published rules. Where the real answer is “it depends on your contract,” we say that rather than guess.

The mechanics, in order

This sequence is set out in the NYS Department of State salesperson licensing guidance. It is not negotiable and it is not something you can do from your side.

  1. Your current broker terminates the association. Until that happens, nothing else can proceed. The Department of State is explicit that termination by the existing representative broker must come before a new firm can perform a change of association. There is no fee for the termination itself.
  2. Your new broker runs “Change Associations.” The incoming principal broker does this from their own licence menu in eAccessNY. The fee is $20 per new association. You do not perform this step and neither does anyone else on your behalf.
  3. Your MLS and association memberships are separate again. They do not travel automatically with the licence change. See the MLS section below.
Why this matters more than it sounds. Because step one belongs to the brokerage you are leaving, a broker who is slow to act can leave you unable to work. Nothing in the licensing process routes around that. It is worth knowing before you plan a start date — and worth asking your current agreement what, if anything, it says about the timing of a release.

The deadline almost nobody mentions

If you hold a lockbox device, a clock starts the day your affiliation ends.

The OneKey® MLS Rules and Procedures require an access device to be returned within the earlier of 48 hours of a request, or five working days after — in the rulebook’s own words — “the termination of any Subscriber’s affiliation with a Participant for any reason.” The device remains the property of the MLS or the manufacturer and may not be transferred to anyone.

This obligation binds you directly, not just your broker — and it is one of very few rules in the whole rulebook that does. It is also not in the lockbox vendor’s FAQ. It exists only in the rulebook, which is why agents routinely do not know about it.

What happens to your listings, your pendings and your clients

Here is the finding that surprised us most when we went through the rulebooks properly.

The public MLS rulebooks are largely silent about the departing salesperson. We put five questions to that layer — listing ownership, MLS access during the gap, signage and lockboxes, client notification, and data. It squarely answers one of them: the lockbox. Nearly every other duty we found runs to the broker, not to the agent.

What that means in practice is that most of what you are worried about is not settled by a rule you can look up. It is settled by your independent contractor agreement. Even the REALTOR® ethics code points in that direction — it bars inducing your current firm’s clients to cancel, then expressly leaves the assignability of exclusive agreements to agreements between brokers and their own licensees.

On your client list, specifically. A New York regulation on brokers and salespersons — 19 NYCRR 175.14 — requires a salesperson on termination to turn over to the broker all listing information obtained during the association. As published guidance from the New York State Association of REALTORS® reads it, that covers information the broker gave you, information you copied from the broker’s records, and information you acquired yourself while affiliated. We are citing that regulation as quoted in NYSAR’s own publication rather than from the regulation text, so treat it as a strong signal to get specific advice, not as our legal conclusion.
A trap if you read national or NYC advice. REBNY’s rules contain the one genuinely detailed “former exclusive agent” regime we found anywhere — what you may say to an owner before you leave, and what you may not do afterwards. It is NYC-only. If you are a Queens or Long Island agent operating under OneKey, it does not govern you. Separately, the only state-specific switching walkthrough we could find on video is for California. Advice written for another state is worse than no advice here.

The Queens MLS question

Queens is the part of this that national content gets wrong, because Queens is genuinely unusual.

Queens straddles two systems: the REBNY Residential Listing Service, which dominates in the Manhattan-adjacent neighbourhoods, and OneKey® MLS, which dominates further out. An agent is required to join neither. That is why Queens inventory fragments across two places, and why the question “which MLS does your brokerage pay for” is a four-figure annual question rather than an administrative detail.

Ask any brokerage you are talking to which MLS they carry, whether they cover the new-subscriber start-up fee, and what happens if your business crosses the line between the two. Also worth knowing: the Long Island Board of REALTORS® covers Queens, Nassau and Suffolk, but not Brooklyn — so if you work across that boundary, ask specifically.

What to ask before you give notice

Because most of the above turns out to be contract rather than law, these are the questions that actually determine how your move goes. Take them to your current agreement first, and then to whoever you are considering.

  • What does my agreement say about commission on deals that are under contract when I leave?
  • What does it say about who releases my listings, and on what timetable?
  • Whose name is on my client database, my CRM, my email address and my domains?
  • Is there anything in it that restricts where I may go, or who I may contact afterwards?
  • Do I owe anything on departure — unreimbursed costs, fees, chargebacks on paid-out commission?
  • Which MLS and which association am I in, what do they cost, and when do they renew?
  • Where is my lockbox device, and what is the return deadline from the day I leave?
