For producing agents

You already know how to do this job.

So this page will not explain real estate to you, and it will not tell you that you are undervalued where you are. You can judge that.

What it will do is be specific about what changes operationally if you move, what we actually offer, and what we do not.

Let us deal with the compensation question first

It is the first thing you want to know, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

We do not publish our commission terms on this website. Not as a negotiating tactic — it is because the terms depend on what you bring, what you need from the brokerage, and what you are doing today. A number on a web page would be either meaningless or misleading.

What we will commit to is this: ask, and you get a straight answer from the supervising broker early in the conversation, not after three meetings. If the answer does not work for you, that is a perfectly good outcome and you have lost an hour.

Being fair about the trade-off, with the research behind it. We are not going to pretend the split is a secondary concern. In Redfin’s 2025 industry survey — 500 agents outside Redfin, sampled from MLS records of agents who closed at least one deal, fielded by Ipsos — commission split was rated “very important” by 78.4%, against 55.2% for brand and reputation and 54.6% for training and support. More pointedly, 54.8% said they would rather their brokerage invested less in training, marketing and technology if it improved their split. That is a competitor’s survey and it is self-reported, but it is the most methodologically solid number we found, and it does not flatter us. Some brokerages publish their economics openly and you can evaluate them without speaking to anyone. We do not. If that is your deciding factor, weigh it against us honestly — we would rather say so here than have you find out in week three.

What actually changes operationally

The mechanics of a New York move are more constrained than most agents realise, and almost none of it is in the brokerage’s gift.

  • You cannot move your own licence. Your current broker has to terminate the association before any new firm can pick you up. That step belongs to the brokerage you are leaving.
  • Your listings sit with your current brokerage, not with you. Moving one needs your broker’s consent, your seller’s consent, and fresh paperwork at the new firm.
  • Your pendings, your database, your phone number and your domains are governed by your agreement, not by law or by any MLS rule we could find. We looked specifically, and the rulebooks are quiet on all of it.
  • Your lockbox device is due back within five working days of your affiliation ending. That one is a rule, it binds you personally, and it is not in the vendor’s documentation.

We have written the whole thing up, with sources, including a table of which of the common worries are settled by law and which by your contract. It is free and it is useful regardless of where you end up.

The one number that actually favours a firm this size

We would rather show you a figure that supports the case than assert the case without one.

NAR’s October 2026 analysis of its own firm survey reports that the share of firms naming agent retention as their biggest challenge over the next two years rises steeply with size: 8% at firms with 1–10 licensees, 45% at 26–99, and 53% at 500 or more. The same analysis notes that nearly a quarter of agents with six to ten years’ experience joined their current firm within the last year — the most mobile bracket among established agents.

Read that honestly and it is not a claim that we are better. It is evidence that agents leave large firms at a rate large firms themselves are worried about, and that something about scale is the cause. The usual explanation offered by the brokers in that research is distance from the people who make decisions. At a firm our size that distance does not exist — which is a structural fact rather than a programme, and the only reason we lead with it.

Source. National Association of REALTORS®, “Agent Movement and Retention Trends,” 16 October 2026, drawing on the 2025 Profile of Real Estate Firms (n=4,672, 2.2% response rate) and the 2026 Member Profile (n=5,116). Both are member surveys of self-selected respondents, not transaction records. Retrieved October 2026.

What we are, and what we are not

A producing agent can smell a padded benefits list. Here is ours without the padding.

What we can tell you truthfully

  • We are an independent, family-owned New York brokerage with an office in Ozone Park, Queens — NYS Principal Office licence #109926909, supervising broker Vinod K. Gadura.
  • You would deal directly with the supervising broker. There is no recruiting layer and no regional structure between you and the person accountable for your licence.
  • We are a OneKey® MLS IDX participant and we run a substantial consumer-facing website across Queens and Long Island.
  • We know the Queens operating environment in detail — the two-MLS problem, the co-op-heavy inventory, the attorney-state closing process — and the guides on this site are the evidence, not a claim about it.

What we are not claiming

  • A lead programme. We do not promise leads in any volume. Nobody can honestly promise a licensed agent that.
  • A formal training or mentorship programme. We have not documented one, so we are not going to advertise one. Ask what support would actually look like for you specifically.
  • A marketing budget, a CRM product or a technology stack as an agent benefit. Ask what exists today, and judge the answer.
  • Production figures, agent counts, awards or testimonials. We have not verified them to a standard we would publish, so they are not here.

Teams and broker-level conversations

If you lead a team, or you are a licensed broker considering an affiliation, we have not built a page for you — deliberately.

Anything we published about team structures, brand treatment or broker-level arrangements would be describing terms that are not settled. Rather than write a page that sounds like an offer and is not one, the honest answer is a direct conversation with the supervising broker about what is and is not possible. Use the form and say what you are proposing.

No application, nothing to sign, and no follow-up sequence. Tell us what you are weighing and we will give you straight answers — including on compensation.
